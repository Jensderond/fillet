# Fillet UI design review (2026-10-24)

Reviewer lens: `swiftui-design-skill` (5-dimension review, anti-slop rules, type/colour/spacing references), with `emil-design-eng` and `apple-design` as secondary lenses for interaction feel. This document is the brief for the next agent. Each finding gives the place to change it, the change itself, and the risk.

## How this review was done

- I built the Mac app from source at `609b7ae` and ran the `FILLET_AUTOMATION=1` scenario in `FILLET_APPEARANCE=light` and `=dark`. Both runs passed all checks.
- Each step's window capture was composited with its viewport render using a chroma key. Inside the Metal rect at (252, 76) pt, chrome-coloured pixels take the renderer's pixel and overlays keep the window's pixel. The composites are in the scratchpad (paths at the end). Screenshot names below use the automation names, for example `light/03-extrude-editing`.
- Not shown by the scenario, so reviewed from code: the browser with parameters, timeline states (failed, suppressed, rolled back, drop target), toasts, the export and printer menus, the iPad layout, the hardware keyboard, and Dynamic Type. The iPad simulator was not usable on this machine (CoreSimulator is out of date). **iPad findings come from reading the code and doing the arithmetic. Verify them in the simulator before and after fixing.**
- Capture caveats, so nobody "fixes" what isn't broken:
  1. `DebugAutomation.snap` calls `ViewportRenderer.snapshot(...)` without `appearance:`, so it defaults to `.light`. Every `dark/*-viewport.png` therefore shows a light viewport. The live app follows `colorScheme` (`ViewportView.swift:45-47`), so this is a harness artefact (see F38).
  2. `cacheDisplay` doesn't capture the title bar or window toolbar, so Undo, Redo, Printer and Export (`DocumentToolbar`) don't appear in any capture.
  3. Around the sketch toolbar, the shadow keeps the chrome colour under it, which shows up as a grey halo in the composites. That halo is a compositing artefact and isn't in the app.

## Design review: Fillet editor (Mac and iPad)

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Philosophy | 8/10 | Coherent "Functional / drafting" school: navy ink on cool neutrals, SF Pro Condensed tool labels, a ruler timeline with a caliper marker, and a graph-paper icon and splash. Without the name you'd still recognise it as one product. It loses points where the dark chrome drifts from the brand navy, and where orange leaks out of its "active" meaning. |
| Visual hierarchy | 6/10 | The inspector reads well (title, then picked-state card, fields, then a pinned Done). The tool strip is 14 equal-weight items with no primary. | Craft quality | 6/10 | Motion tokens and press states are disciplined. | Originality | 8/10 | The ruler timeline with its caliper, the hatched rolled-back steps, the dimension-line live measures and the splash's fillet pass are real signature details. Of the anti-slop list, only the tinted "icon in a soft square" chip reads slightly generic. |
| **Average** | **6.6/10** | |

**Verdict:** distinctive, well-founded and close to shippable on Mac. It doesn't yet meet the skill's ship threshold (every dimension at 7 or above), and on iPad it isn't shippable. The identity is right. What's missing is iPad ergonomics, accessibility (Dynamic Type, contrast, VoiceOver for the timeline) and stricter use of the two accents. **Don't redesign; tighten.** Batches 1 to 3 of the plan below should bring Craft and Functionality to 7 or above.

### What's working well (keep as is)

1. **The ruler timeline.** Stations on a graduated ruler, an orange caliper rollback marker, section-view hatching past the marker, suppressed steps with a dashed border and strikethrough, a red failure dot, and an orange drop-target bar (`TimelineView.swift`). This is the app's best detail. Polish it; don't replace it with a generic list or chips.
2. **The two-accent idea itself.** Orange means active and blue means picked. In the tool strip (active tool: tinted fill, orange glyph and a 2.5 pt underline; view toggles go ink, not orange; `EditorView.swift:327-338`) and in the timeline (station being edited), it's executed exactly right. The fixes below extend it; they don't change it.
3. **The `SelectionCount` well.** A dashed empty well ("Pick edges") turns into a blue-tinted filled card ("4 edges picked · Clear"), matching the viewport's selection colour (`light/05-fillet-editing`). It's clear, honest feedback.
4. **The inspector anatomy.** The header and actions stay put while the fields scroll, and Cancel/Done is pinned with a full-width orange Done (`InspectorView.swift:7-31, 81-101`).
5. **Live dimensions while drawing.** The offsets are drawn like real drawing dimension lines, with small pills (`light/02a-drawing`, `ViewportBridge.swift:167-188`). It's very CAD-native.
6. **The hint pill** ("Tap a corner") in ink, top-centre, crossfading with a 2 pt blur, marked `.updatesFrequently` (`EditorView.swift:197-210`).
7. **The motion system.** `Theme.Motion` uses a 0.97 press scale, strong ease-out, exits faster than entrances, and no animation on tool switches (`EditorView.swift:418`). Panel transitions honour Reduce Motion. The marker animation is turned off under Reduce Motion.
8. **The "Updating" label only appears after 180 ms** (`EditorView.swift:104-109`). That's the right call for perceived speed.
9. **The print check card.** Fits the bed, overhangs and watertight, with a shape-coded icon per state (not colour only), plus material grams (`light/04-extruded`). It's on-brand for 3D printing.
10. **The splash** (`docs/splash/frames.png`, `SplashOverlay.swift`). It doesn't block: any input skips it, it has a reduced-motion still, and it's hidden from accessibility. It's a model of how to do a launch moment.
11. **The icon.** The flat graph-paper isometric drawing with the orange fillet face is legible at 16 px.
12. **`ExprField` states.** Rest, hover, focus (orange ring), error (red border plus reason, input not applied) and a computed "= 30 mm" preview for expressions. The behaviour is right; only the sizes need adjusting.
13. **Typeface choices.** System SF Pro with SF Pro Condensed for technical labels. The skill's "serif display" suggestion does **not** apply to a technical CAD UI (the skill itself says not to use serif for data-heavy or technical UIs), so don't add New York.

### Conflicts and decisions to make

- **C1. Touch targets (F02) vs portrait width (F01).** 44 pt sketch tools make the floating toolbar about 690 pt wide. Ship them with the `ViewThatFits` compact variant and the sketch footer (F41), never alone.
- **C2. Always-visible inspector (F06) vs narrow layouts (F01).** "Always visible" applies only at 1100 pt or wider. Below that, the current conditional rule applies, plus the overlay or sheet behaviour.
- **C3. Primary button colour (F04).** Option A (recommended) is a `filletFill` of `#C94F0B` with a white label (4.56:1), which reads as burnt orange; the caliper and accents stay `#F26B1D`. Option B keeps `#F26B1D` with a navy `#1D2B45` label (4.65:1), which is brighter but less conventional. Pick one with the owner and apply it to every `PrimaryButtonStyle` use. Don't mix them.
- **C4. Construction-line colour (Batch 5).** Fusion users expect orange dashed construction lines, but here orange means "active". Recommendation: a dashed slate colour. Ask the owner if unsure. Either way, the preview stroke must stop being selection blue.
- **C5. `inkTertiary` retune vs hierarchy.** Darkening it for contrast narrows the gap to `inkSecondary`. That's why the plan moves **text** to `inkSecondary` and keeps `inkTertiary` for non-text marks only. Don't just darken `inkTertiary` and leave the text on it.
- **C6. `.inspector(isPresented:)` vs the hand-rolled `HStack` (F01).** The system modifier gives free compact adaptation, but it changes toolbar placement and the transitions. Moving to it is a separate change; don't combine it with Batch 3's minimal approach.
- **C7. Dark neutrals toward navy (F26) vs the viewport's dark palette.** The viewport background (`#30343B` to `#16181C`) is neutral. If the chrome goes navy, shift `backgroundTop` and `backgroundBottom` by the same hue (for example `#2E3340` to `#151922`), or the seam at the viewport edge will show.
