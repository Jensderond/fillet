Two colours tell you what's going on. **Orange** is the step you're working on. **Blue** is what you've picked.

## Sketch the outline

Every part starts as a flat drawing. Here it's an 80 × 30 mm rectangle on the bed.

<div class="split">
<div>

1. Open Fillet and tap **New Design**.
2. Tap <Ui icon="sketching">Sketch</Ui> in the tool strip, then tap **XY** (the bed) in the bar that appears. The view turns to look straight down.
3. Tap <Ui>Rectangle</Ui> in the sketch bar and drag from corner to corner with Apple Pencil.
4. Tap <Ui>Select</Ui> (the arrow), tap the rectangle, and type **80** in **Width** and **30** in **Height**.
5. Tap **Finish sketch**.

In landscape, **Start a sketch on the bed** in the inspector does step 2 in one tap.

No Pencil? Tap the hand button in the sketch bar, just left of **Finish sketch** (in portrait it's the bar's last button). Your finger then draws instead of turning the view.

</div>
<Figure src="/screens/getting-started/step-sketch.png" width={638} height={830} alt="The sketch inspector for Sketch 1 with the rectangle selected: Rectangle set to Corner, Width 80 mm and Height 30 mm" caption="Draw roughly, then type the real sizes." />
</div>

<Tip title="Type the sizes">Fillet has no constraint solver. Each shape keeps the sizes you type, and a field also takes a sum or a [parameter](/guides/parameters/), such as `width / 2`.</Tip>

## Make it solid

Extrude pushes the sketch up into a plate.

<div class="split">
<div>

1. Tap <Ui icon="extrude-revolve-press-pull">Extrude</Ui>. The rectangle is the only shape, so it's picked for you.
2. Type **6** in **Distance**, or drag the orange arrow on the part.
3. Tap **Done**.

</div>
<Figure src="/screens/getting-started/step-extrude.png" width={638} height={560} alt="The Extrude 1 inspector: 1 profile picked, Operation New, Distance 6 mm" caption="The arrow and Distance set the same thing." />
</div>

## Round the corners

Fillet rounds edges. Round the four upright corners so the plate has no sharp points.

<div class="split">
<div>

1. Tap <Ui icon="fillet-chamfer-shell">Fillet</Ui>.
2. Tap each of the four upright corner edges. For the one at the back, drag with one finger to turn the view first.
3. Type **5** in **Radius**.
4. Tap **Done**.

The part shows the round as you type, so you see it before you tap **Done**.

</div>
<Figure src="/screens/getting-started/step-fillet.png" width={638} height={560} alt="The Fillet 1 inspector: 4 edges picked in 4 tangent chains, Tangent chain on, Radius 5 mm" caption="Each tap adds one edge to the count." />
</div>

## Drill the screw holes

Hole drills holes that fit a real screw. Pick the screw, and Fillet fills in the sizes.

<div class="split">
<div>

1. Tap <Ui icon="holes">Hole</Ui>.
2. Tap the top of the plate near each end. The first tap picks the face, and each tap adds a hole.
3. Tap **Preset** and choose **Socket head counterbore** › **M3**.
4. Under **Extent**, tap **Through all**.
5. Tap **Done**.

</div>
<Figure src="/screens/getting-started/step-hole.png" width={638} height={624} alt="The Hole 1 inspector: 2 holes picked, Preset M3 socket head counterbore, and a hint that it adds the clearance parameter of 0.2 mm" caption="Screw holes get 0.2 mm of clearance." />
</div>

If your screws bind once printed, raise `clearance` under **Parameters** in the browser, and every hole follows. [Tune the fit for your printer →](/guides/holes/#tune-the-fit-for-your-printer)

## Send it to your slicer

With nothing selected, the inspector shows **Print check**. Three green ticks mean the part is ready to print.

<div class="split">
<div>

1. Check that Print check says **Fits the bed**, **No overhangs** and **Watertight**.
2. Tap <Ui>Export</Ui> in the toolbar and choose **3MF**.
3. Save the file to Files, then open it in Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer or your slicer of choice.

That's a printable part, about 17 g of PLA. Every step stays on the timeline: tap **Fillet 1** there, type a new radius and tap **Done**, and the steps after it update.

</div>
<Figure src="/screens/getting-started/step-export.png" width={638} height={1100} alt="The Export menu open over the inspector with 3MF (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer…), STL and STEP (other CAD apps). Under it, Print check shows Fits the bed 80 × 30 × 6 mm, No overhangs and Watertight, and Material 17.36 g of PLA" caption="Export sits at the top right. 3MF opens in most slicers." />
</div>

## Questions and answers

<FAQ>
  <QA q="How do I move around the model?">Drag with one finger to turn it, drag with two fingers to slide it, and pinch to zoom. Double-tap with two fingers to fit everything in view. With Apple Pencil, you draw and pick, and your fingers keep moving the view. On a keyboard's trackpad, scroll with two fingers to slide and pinch to zoom.</QA>
  <QA q="Why is a tool grey?">It can't run yet. Tap it anyway and Fillet says why. In an empty design, **Fillet** says *Needs a body. Extrude a sketch first.*</QA>
  <QA q="How do I change how the part looks?">Use the buttons under the view cube. Tap a face of the cube for a straight-on view, tap <Ui>Fit all</Ui> to frame the part, and use the chip under them to pick one of six styles, from **Shaded** to **Hidden line**. They change the view, never the design.</QA>
  <QA q="Do I need an account?">No. Fillet works offline, never asks you to sign in and never needs a network.</QA>
  <QA q="Where are my designs?">Wherever you save them: On My iPad, iCloud Drive or any Files location. Each design is one `.fillet` file. [Files, iCloud and importing STEP →](/guides/files-and-icloud/)</QA>
  <QA q="What changes in portrait or Split View?">The panels make room for the part. The browser floats over it: tap <Ui>Browser</Ui> in the tool strip to show it. The inspector appears once you start a step or pick something, and in the narrowest windows it's a drawer under the view that you drag taller or shorter. An empty design has no **Start a sketch on the bed** button there, so tap <Ui icon="sketching">Sketch</Ui>, then **XY**.</QA>
  <QA q="Does it work the same on a Mac?">Yes. Click where this guide says tap, and drag to turn the view. On a trackpad, scroll with two fingers to slide the view, and pinch or hold <Keys combo="⌘" /> while you scroll to zoom. With a mouse, the scroll wheel zooms and Shift-right-drag slides. Press <Keys combo="⌘0" /> to fit everything in view.</QA>
</FAQ>
